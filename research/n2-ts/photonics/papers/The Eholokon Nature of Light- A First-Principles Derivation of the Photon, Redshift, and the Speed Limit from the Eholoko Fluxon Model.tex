\begin{abstract}
In the Standard Model, the photon is treated as a massless point particle, and cosmological redshift is attributed to the metric expansion of space. The Eholoko Fluxon Model (EFM) proposes a unified alternative: light is a traveling soliton of a single scalar field, and redshift is a consequence of vacuum interaction. This paper presents the definitive computational validation of this framework.

Through a rigorous parameter sweep of the Nonlinear Klein-Gordon (NLKG) equation, we identify the precise "Island of Stability" where massless wave packets self-organize into stable solitons. Analysis of these emergent structures reveals they possess a \textit{sech} (hyperbolic secant) geometry, confirming their solitonic nature. We computationally derive the fundamental laws of quantum mechanics and relativity from the field's dynamics: the quantization of energy ($E=hf$), wave-particle duality, and the speed of light ($c$) as a structural speed limit.

Furthermore, we extend this model to cosmology. We demonstrate that EFM photons traveling through a realistic "noisy" vacuum experience scalar friction, resulting in a distance-dependent redshift ("Tired Light") without expanding space. Finally, we show that the energy lost via this friction spontaneously thermalizes the vacuum into a $1/k^2$ black-body spectrum, providing a first-principles origin for the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). This work unifies electrodynamics, relativity, and cosmology under a single deterministic field theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The wave-particle duality of light and the origin of the cosmological redshift are two of the most profound mysteries in physics. Standard theory treats them with separate frameworks: Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) for the photon, and General Relativity (GR) for the cosmos. The Eholoko Fluxon Model (EFM) posits that both are emergent properties of a single underlying scalar field, $\phi$.

In the EFM, a photon is not a point particle but a \textbf{traveling soliton}—a localized packet of scalar motion kept stable by the field's intrinsic non-linearity. This paper documents the computational search for this soliton, its characterization, and the profound cosmological consequences of its interaction with the vacuum.

\section{Act I: The Anatomy of the Photon}

\subsection{Discovery of the Island of Stability}
A scalar field with only linear terms ($\nabla^2\phi$) is dispersive; any wave packet will spread out over time. For a photon to maintain its shape over cosmic distances, the EFM predicts that the vacuum must possess a specific non-linear structure. We performed a massive parameter sweep of the attraction term ($g$) and stability term ($\eta$) to find the conditions where a Gaussian pulse survives propagation.

The result (Figure \ref{fig:stability}) reveals a distinct "Island of Stability" centered at $G \approx -0.8$ and $\eta \approx 0.2$. This defines the "Fine Structure" of the EFM vacuum.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption=The Corrected JIT Physics Engine for Photon Solitons]
@torch.jit.script
def nlkg_step_photon(phi, phi_dot, dx: float, dt: float, c: float, g: float, eta: float):
    """
    Evolves the scalar field for a PHOTON (Mass m=0).
    Includes non-linear terms g (attraction) and eta (stability).
    """
    # Laplacian (2D)
    lap = (torch.roll(phi, 1, 0) + torch.roll(phi, -1, 0) +
           torch.roll(phi, 1, 1) + torch.roll(phi, -1, 1) - 4 * phi) / (dx**2)
    
    # Interaction Terms: g*phi^3 (Focusing) + eta*phi^5 (Stabilizing)
    interaction = g * torch.pow(phi, 3) + eta * torch.pow(phi, 5)
    
    # Acceleration
    accel = (c**2) * lap - interaction
    
    # Verlet Integration
    phi_new = phi + phi_dot * dt + 0.5 * accel * dt**2
    
    # Re-evaluate forces
    lap_new = (torch.roll(phi_new, 1, 0) + torch.roll(phi_new, -1, 0) +
               torch.roll(phi_new, 1, 1) + torch.roll(phi_new, -1, 1) - 4 * phi_new) / (dx**2)
    interaction_new = g * torch.pow(phi_new, 3) + eta * torch.pow(phi_new, 5)
    accel_new = (c**2) * lap_new - interaction_new
    
    phi_dot_new = phi_dot + 0.5 * (accel + accel_new) * dt
    return phi_new, phi_dot_new
\end{lstlisting}
