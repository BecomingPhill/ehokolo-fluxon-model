Understanding the Eholoko Fluxon Model (EFM)

Unlike traditional molecular docking applications (like AutoDock Vina, Schrödinger, or Amber) which use classical mechanics force fields or computationally intensive quantum chemistry approximations (such as Hartree-Fock or Density Functional Theory), **Flux Chem Studio** operates directly on the principles of first-principles wave thermodynamics.

The Wave Hypothesis: EFM treats subatomic and molecular interfaces as continuous scalar wave fields (represented by ψ) governed by a modified Non-Linear Klein-Gordon (NLKG) equation. Covalent and ionic structures represent stationary resonant harmonics where these waves settle into stable configurations.

In typical quantum simulations, computing the anti-symmetric multi-electron wavefunction requires evaluating O(N!) Slater determinants. This scaling barrier makes interactive, real-time quantum docking impossible on modern CPUs. The EFM paradigm circumvents this limitation by representing multi-particle structures as field densities. Matter wave interactions are solved iteratively in 3D grid space via the Verlet wave relaxation algorithm, delivering quantum-like accuracy in milliseconds.

The Biochemical Translation Bridge

To ensure researchers are not slowed down by EFM physics terminology, Flux Chem Studio maps every simulation variable to its traditional biochemistry counterpart.

EFM Physics Concept Traditional Biochemical Term Biophysical Role & Meaning
Harmonic Density State (HDS) Atomic Shell Density Zone Discrete concentric density bands (ρ = k|ψ|2) surrounding nuclei where waves accumulate.
HDS 1 (Core) Atomic Nucleus / Core Electrons Very high density region. Strongly repulsive to prevent other nuclei from occupying the same space (Pauli repulsion).
HDS 2 (Mantle) Valence Electron / Covalent Bond Moderate density region. Overlapping mantles of adjacent atoms form stable shared harmonics (covalent bonds).
HDS 3 (Binding) Electrostatic & Hydrogen Bonds Low density tails extending outward. Dictates weaker non-covalent overlaps, including ionic attraction and hydrogen bonds.
Specific Phase Friction (Espec) Wave Gradient Energy Quantifies the smoothness of the matter wave interface. A high-gradient clashing wave has high phase friction; a smooth overlap has low friction.
Friction Shift (ΔE) Relative Binding Energy / Score Analogous to Free Energy Change (ΔG). Calculated as: ΔE = Ecomplex - Etarget. A negative shift indicates stable binding; positive indicates steric clash.
Lability Index (Lsol) Dynamical Soliton Lability Measures matter wave stability under Langevin thermal noise perturbation. A low lability index indicates a rigid Blocker; a moderate one indicates an Activator.
Verlet Wave Relaxation Geometry Optimization Iterative numerical integration that dampens kinetic energy to find the lowest energy conformation of the wave fields.

Biophysical Simulation Parameters

You can tune the local EFM solver using the sliders in the control panel. Changing these variables changes the resolution, speed, and accuracy of your simulations:

1. Grid Resolution (N³)

Defines the number of grid points along each axis of the 3D grid. A resolution of 32 results in a 32 × 32 × 32 grid (32,768 simulation nodes).

Optimization Tip: Use `32` for virtual screening of compound libraries. Increase to `48` or `64` for final high-accuracy binding score calculations of highly polar active site pockets.

2. Box Size (L, Å)

The physical length of the simulation volume in Angstroms, centered on the target binding pocket.

* Larger values (e.g. 24Å - 32Å) are required for larger ligands, such as cyclic peptides or branched compounds, to prevent clipping. * Smaller values (e.g. 8Å - 12Å) focus all computational resolution onto the core catalytic residues, accelerating runtimes.

3. Simulation Steps

The total number of time steps the Verlet solver is allowed to execute during field relaxation.

* If the energy score is still fluctuating heavily when the simulation ends, increase the steps. * If the energy score stabilizes early (flatlines), you can reduce the steps to accelerate throughput.

4. Damping (delta)

The kinetic damping coefficient. It acts as a friction coefficient that removes kinetic energy from the scalar fields, forcing the wave to settle into its static ground state.

Stability Warning: Setting the damping too low (e.g. < 0.1) can cause waves to oscillate indefinitely, preventing convergence. Setting it too high (e.g. > 0.4) might converge too quickly, trapping the system in a local metadata bottleneck rather than a global minimum.

5. Low-Spec Hardware Mode

Enable this option if you are running calculations on dual-core or quad-core consumer laptops. It downscales the resolution to 24³ and restricts the solver thread pool to leave one CPU core free, ensuring the desktop GUI remains responsive during simulation runs.

Tutorial: Running a Molecular Docking Assay

Let's walk through how to execute a virtual screening of a target-ligand interface step-by-step:

  1. Structure Setup: In the PDB input box, enter a PDB ID (e.g. 1HSG for HIV-1 Protease) and click Fetch PDB, or select a pre-calibrated template from the **Quick-Load NTD Target Template** dropdown (e.g. *Plasmodium falciparum* DHFR `1J3J` or *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* InhA `1ZID`).
  2. Load Ligand: Switch the Ligand source dropdown to search PubChem online or load directly from the cached **African Natural Product Library** (containing 15 active phytocompounds isolated from local plants like Artemisinin or Cryptolepine, working fully offline).
  3. Execute Docking: Click Run EFM Docking. The background FastAPI server feeds the structure to the PyTorch engine, centers the simulation grid, and relaxes the fields.
  4. Inspect Scores: The Biophysical Scores panel updates:
    • Target Phase Energy (EA): Empty active pocket friction.
    • Complex Phase Energy (EAB): Combined protein-ligand friction.
    • Relative Binding Score (ΔE): The final binding shift. If ΔE is negative, a stable, low-friction wave overlap has succeeded! If positive, a steric clash occurred.
    • Lability Index (Lsol): Langevin-perturbed wave stability indicating active-site target binding mode (Blocker vs Activator).
  5. Synergy Docking: Toggle **Synergy Mode**, load up to 3 compounds sequentially, click **Add to Pool** for each, and click **Run Synergy Docking** to analyze cooperative multi-ligand binding of phytochemical synergy pools.

De Novo Fragment Evolution (Shz-1 Pipeline)

The **Shz-1 pipeline** handles autonomous scaffold design inside empty pockets. Instead of searching a pre-existing library of chemicals, it dynamically constructs molecules by inserting carbon atom mutations:

  • An initial **oxygen seed** is placed at the catalytic center.
  • The solver evaluates potential carbon mutations along multiple coordinate axes.
  • For each potential scaffold, the EFM solver calculates the wave overlap friction shift (ΔE).
  • Scaffolds resulting in a positive shift (ΔE > 0) are identified as clashing with the pocket walls and rejected.
  • Scaffolds resulting in a negative shift (ΔE < 0) are selected, and the best-performing scaffold is rendered in the 3D viewer.

FastAPI Solver API Endpoints

If you are building external bioinformatics scripts or running automated pipelines, you can hit the local FastAPI server directly:

  • POST /fetch_target: Fetch a target structure by PDB ID.
  • POST /fetch_ligand: Fetch a ligand structure by PubChem name.
  • GET /natural_products: Get list of cached African natural product library molecules.
  • GET /fetch_natural_product/{id}: Fetch cached natural product details and SDF coordinates.
  • GET /ntd_templates: Get list of pre-calibrated NTD target templates.
  • POST /run_screening: Run EFM screening on target and ligand atoms.
  • POST /run_synergy_screening: Run EFM synergy screening on target and multiple ligands.
  • POST /run_evolution: Run Shz-1 de novo evolution.

8. 100-Target Statistical Benchmarking & Validation Engine

To establish scientific credibility and satisfy stringent pharmaceutical peer-review guidelines, Flux Chem Studio includes an embedded **100-Target Statistical Validation Engine**. This engine performs offline biophysical screening assays on a diverse library of 100 targets compiled from the PDBbind refined set, spanning multiple target classes (e.g. Kinases, Proteases, GPCRs, Nuclear Receptors).

For each target, the solver runs EFM matter wave simulations to calculate the Specific Phase Friction shift (ΔE). It then fits a linear regression calibration to map predictions to experimental inhibition constants (pKi) to calculate global Pearson correlation (r), Spearman rank correlation (ρ), the statistical p-value (p), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE).

⚠️ Biophysical Category Error Warning (Global Pooling Washout)

Running a global linear calibration across all 100 targets yields a misleading correlation (r ≈ 0.023, p ≈ 0.82). This is a physical category error: the Specific Phase Friction shift (ΔE) is highly dependent on pocket-specific boundary conditions, such as pocket volume, residue density, and target electrostatic potentials.

Calibrating class-by-class adjusts for these pocket-dependent matter wave friction scales, revealing EFM's true predictive strength within individual target classes (e.g., Nuclear Receptors: r ≈ 0.99, DHFR: r ≈ 0.99). Stratified calibration reduces Mean Absolute Error (MAE) from 0.85 to 0.38 log units.
